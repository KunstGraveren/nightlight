A lithophane is a three-dimensional (3D) relief image that reveals its detailed picture when backlit by a light source. It is created by varying the thickness of the material. Thicker areas of the lithophane block more light, appearing darker, while thinner areas allow more light to pass through, appearing brighter. A lithophane is produced by converting a 2D image into a 3D model where pixel brightness levels are translated into corresponding thicknesses. This technique leverages the relationship between light transmission and material thickness to produce detailed, visually compelling images in 3D printed form.

## How will this translate to how it's printed on a 3D Printer?

<img width="1932" height="854" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/88a21a12-3cbb-4e55-9d4d-34cd54506d90" />

Your cylinder will be “sliced” into “layers (above you see 1 layer of the cylinder). Here u can also clearly see the difference where dents that have been made on the spots where the brightness was the highest.

The cylinder is been printed with a “line” of 0.4mm. Because the “Max Thick” is set to 2mm we end up with 5 lines to make the width of 2mm. (In the picture you can see 2 orange, 2 yellow and 1 white line)

So, bottom line.. Black will be near 0.8 value, while White will be more against the 2 value, which will translate in more or less light that is being passed through the cylinder.

You can adjust these settings, but don't go below 0,8 for “Min Thick” also don't make it to “Max Thick” too high, then there will be no light through it.
